# Oracle

**Oracle Corporation** is a major American enterprise-technology company associated with database software, enterprise computing, cloud infrastructure, Java, and enterprise software.

> **Project note:** This document recognizes Oracle's technical and historical significance. It does not imply that Senior.Senate.Attorney.E44Hrs is an Oracle product or that Oracle endorses this project.

## Founder — Larry Ellison

Larry Ellison founded Oracle in **1977** and served as its CEO until September 2014. Oracle identifies him as its Executive Chairman of the Board and Chief Technology Officer.

## Corporate Development

Oracle's history includes Oracle Database, enterprise applications and middleware, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, Java, engineered systems, developer tooling, and technologies brought into Oracle through major acquisitions, including Sun Microsystems. Oracle acquired Sun Microsystems in 2010, bringing Java and other technologies into Oracle's portfolio.

## Buildings, Facilities, and Infrastructure

As of May 31, 2026, Oracle reported approximately **0.9 million square feet** at its Austin headquarters, **25.4 million square feet** of other office facilities, and approximately **181 global data-center locations**. It reported approximately **$21.263 billion** in gross buildings and improvements and **$39.973 billion** in construction in progress.

A data-center location is not necessarily a single building, so building counts should not be inferred from location counts.

Oracle's common stock trades on the **New York Stock Exchange** under **ORCL**. Its fiscal-2026 filing also identifies **ORCL-PRD** for its Series D mandatory convertible preferred stock.

## Legal and Corporate Conduct

A responsible corporate history records documented legal and regulatory matters without exaggeration.

In 2011, the U.S. Department of Justice announced that Oracle Corp. and Oracle America Inc. agreed to pay **$199.5 million plus interest** to resolve a False Claims Act lawsuit concerning a General Services Administration software contract.

In 2022, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission announced that Oracle agreed to pay **more than $23 million** to resolve Foreign Corrupt Practices Act charges concerning subsidiaries in Turkey, the United Arab Emirates, and India. These documented civil/regulatory matters should be distinguished from criminal convictions.
